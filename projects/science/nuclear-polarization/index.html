Thermal polarization in NMR

This interactive NMR polarization calculator shows the thermal equilibrium polarization of nuclear spins and electrons as a function of magnetic field and temperature. The graph plots polarization against the ratio of magnetic field to temperature, \(B/T\), allowing different nuclei to be compared directly through their gyromagnetic ratios.

For a spin-\(\tfrac{1}{2}\) particle at thermal equilibrium, the magnitude of the polarization is given by the Boltzmann expression

\[ P = \tanh\left( \frac{\hbar\lvert\gamma\rvert B} {2k_{\mathrm B}T} \right) \]

where \(P\) is the polarization, \(B\) is the magnetic field in tesla, \(T\) is the temperature in kelvin, and \(\gamma\) is the gyromagnetic ratio in rad s\(^{-1}\) T\(^{-1}\). For nuclei with spin \(I>\tfrac{1}{2}\), the calculation uses the corresponding Brillouin function, \[ P = B_I(x) = \frac{2I+1}{2I} \coth\left(\frac{2I+1}{2I}x\right) - \frac{1}{2I} \coth\left(\frac{x}{2I}\right), \qquad x=\frac{\hbar\lvert\gamma\rvert B}{k_{\mathrm B}T}. \] This reduces exactly to the hyperbolic tangent expression for \(I=\tfrac{1}{2}\). Because the polarization depends on \(B/T\), the horizontal axis is expressed in T K\(^{-1}\).

Select one or more nuclei below the graph to compare their thermal polarization. The table gives the gyromagnetic ratio both in rad s\(^{-1}\) T\(^{-1}\) and MHz T\(^{-1}\), while the calculator allows the polarization at a specific magnetic field and temperature to be calculated directly.
